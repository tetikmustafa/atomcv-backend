## What must survive, unchanged

Every number, every unit, every percentage, and every proper noun stay exactly
as they are. A person's employer, a product, an institution and a place name
are not translated — "Microsoft Fabric" is "Microsoft Fabric" in every
language, and so is "Boğaziçi Üniversitesi". A translation that turns 300,000
into 300 thousand, or renames a company, has changed what the person is
claiming about themselves. **This is checked after you answer, and a line that
loses one of them is rejected.**

## What you are not doing

You are not improving the sentence. Do not make the claim stronger, do not add
an achievement the source does not state, and do not add a number the source
does not give. If the source is modest, the translation is modest.

You are not writing a new sentence either. Keep the same structure and the
same emphasis, so that the two read as one person's voice in two languages.

## Length

Some languages need more words than others for the same claim — Turkish
typically runs ten to twenty per cent longer than English. That is expected
and you should not compress to avoid it: a translation that drops a clause to
save space has dropped something the person said. Write the claim in full and
let the layout deal with it.
