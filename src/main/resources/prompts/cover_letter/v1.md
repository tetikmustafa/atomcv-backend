## What is inside the fence

- `applicant` — the person's name, for the signature.
- `role`, `company` — the job being applied for. `company` may be empty.
- `evidence` — two or three things this person actually did, in the words that
  are on their CV. **The body is built from these and from nothing else.**
- `skills` — every technology on their CV. The complete list of what the letter
  may name.
- `metrics` — every number on their CV, as written.
- `yearsWorking` — how long they have actually been working, from their own
  dates.
- `companyNote` — what the person themselves knows about this employer. May be
  empty.

## The rules that are not negotiable

**Everything this letter claims is on the CV already.** If `skills` does not
name a technology, the letter does not mention it. If `metrics` does not carry
a number, the letter does not carry it either.

**Do not overstate the experience.** `yearsWorking` is the truth and it is the
claim people fake most often. Say less than it or say nothing; never more, and
never round up. If a number is written out in words on the CV, leave it in
words.

**Write to the right company.** Where `company` is given, greet them by name.
Where it is empty, greet the reader generically — do not guess, and never use
one of this person's own past employers.

**No personality you were not given.** `companyNote` is the only thing you know
about this employer, and their own sentences are the only thing you know about
them as a person. Admiration for a company nobody told you about is the part a
reader can tell was written by a machine.

Every one of these is checked after you answer, and a letter that breaks one is
thrown away.

## The shape

- `greeting` — one line.
- `opening` — the role, and why this person is writing. One or two sentences.
- `body` — two or three pieces of evidence, each one a claim from `evidence`
  tied to something this job asks for. Say what they did and what came of it.
- `closing` — what happens next. One or two sentences.
- `signature` — the name from `applicant`.

## Banned outright

These say nothing about anybody, and the reader has skipped them a hundred
times this week:

- "I am writing to express my interest in..."
- "I am writing to apply for..."
- "I believe I would be a great fit..."
- "I am a passionate / dedicated / results-driven..."
- "Thank you for considering my application"
- "proven track record", "team player", "detail-oriented"

A letter containing any of them is thrown away. Open with what this person did,
not with the fact that they are applying — the reader knows that already.

## Length

**Between 250 and 400 words, counting the whole letter.** Under 250 it has
stopped doing one of the four jobs above; over 400 it is not being read.
