Senior Software Engineer, Platform Engineering
Meridian Group, Industrial Automation Division, Stuttgart

Our organisation
Meridian Group is a listed engineering and services company employing around
eleven thousand people across nineteen countries. Founded in 1948 as a supplier
of precision bearings, the group now operates four divisions: industrial
automation, energy services, transport infrastructure and a smaller software
business that grew out of our maintenance analytics work. Revenue for the last
financial year was 3.4 billion euro, of which the software division contributed
just under nine percent, the fastest growing share and the reason this position
exists.

The division
Industrial Automation designs, installs and maintains control systems for
manufacturing plants: automotive assembly, food and beverage, pharmaceutical
packaging and, increasingly, battery cell production. A typical project runs
between eighteen months and four years, involves between forty and three hundred
of our people at different stages, and is delivered against a contract whose
penalty clauses are measured in days of plant downtime. The division employs
about two thousand nine hundred people and is headquartered in Stuttgart with
engineering centres in Brno, Pune and Monterrey.

The position
We are seeking a senior software engineer to join the Platform Engineering
group within Industrial Automation. The group builds and maintains the internal
platform on which our project teams deliver customer-facing control and
monitoring software: shared libraries, deployment tooling, the internal package
registry, the observability stack and the test environments that stand in for
plant hardware. The platform is used daily by roughly three hundred and forty
engineers across the three engineering centres, and its availability is measured
and reported to the divisional board each month.

Reporting line and team
The successful candidate will report to the head of platform engineering and
will join a team of eleven engineers, two of whom are based in Brno and one in
Pune. The team operates on a two-week cadence with a written plan published at
the start of each period and a written review at the end. There is no formal
sub-team structure; work is claimed rather than assigned, and the engineer who
claims a piece of work is expected to see it through to the point where somebody
else can maintain it.

Principal accountabilities
The role carries responsibility for the design, implementation, testing,
documentation and ongoing maintenance of platform components. Specifically, the
successful candidate will contribute to the architecture of the internal
deployment tooling, which currently serves around nine hundred releases a month;
maintain and extend the shared communication libraries used by project teams to
speak to programmable logic controllers over OPC UA and Modbus; improve the
hardware-in-the-loop test environment so that a project team can obtain a
representative environment within one working day rather than the current five;
participate in the design review process for customer-facing software, providing
platform perspective without becoming a gatekeeper; and act as second-line
support for platform incidents during European business hours on a rotating
basis, currently one week in six.

Quality and compliance
Our software is deployed in environments where failure has physical
consequences. The successful candidate will be expected to work within our
established engineering quality framework, which is aligned to IEC 61508 for the
safety-related components and to ISO 9001 across the division. This includes
traceability from requirement to test, documented design decisions for anything
that affects a safety function, and participation in the internal audit cycle.
Candidates who have not worked in a regulated environment before should not be
deterred: roughly half of the current team came from unregulated software, and
the framework is taught rather than assumed.

Essential requirements
Applicants must hold a degree in computer science, software engineering,
electrical engineering or a closely related discipline, or be able to
demonstrate equivalent professional experience. A minimum of six years of
professional software development experience is required, of which at least
three must have been in a role with design responsibility rather than
implementation alone. Strong practical knowledge of at least two of the
following is essential: modern C++, C# on the .NET platform, Python, or Go.
Candidates must be able to read and reason about concurrent code and must be
comfortable with version control, code review and continuous integration as
daily practice rather than as policy.

Further essential requirements
The role requires the ability to communicate in written and spoken English
with colleagues who do not share a first language; our working language is
English and all design documentation is written in it. German is useful in
Stuttgart and is not required. Candidates must be eligible to work in Germany or
the Czech Republic without sponsorship, or be willing to relocate with our
assistance. Occasional travel to customer sites is expected, typically between
four and eight trips a year, each of two to four days.

Desirable experience
The following would strengthen an application without being required:
experience with industrial protocols, particularly OPC UA; familiarity with
containerised deployment in environments without reliable internet access;
exposure to hardware-in-the-loop or simulation-based testing; experience
maintaining a library consumed by teams you do not control, and the versioning
discipline that requires; contributions to open source, particularly in the
industrial or embedded space; and any experience of migrating a large codebase
between build systems, which is a piece of work this team expects to undertake
within the next two years.

What we offer
The position carries a salary band of 78,000 to 96,000 euro depending on
experience and location, together with a group profit-share scheme that has paid
out in eleven of the last thirteen years. Benefits include thirty days of annual
leave, a company pension with employer contribution of six percent, a subsidised

Our approach to hiring
We aim to make a decision within fifteen working days of receiving an
application. The process consists of an initial conversation of about forty
minutes with the hiring manager, a technical discussion of ninety minutes with
two engineers from the team based on a piece of code we send you beforehand (we
do not ask candidates to write code under observation), a conversation about
design and trade-offs, and a final conversation with the head of platform
engineering. We reimburse reasonable travel costs for any part of the process
that takes place in person.

A typical project
To give a sense of the work, here is a project the group completed last year.
A tier-one automotive supplier asked us to replace the control software on four
body-in-white welding lines without stopping production for more than a single
weekend shift per line. The platform contribution was the simulation
environment: a model of each line accurate enough that the project team could
run the new control logic against it for six weeks before anybody went near the
plant. Two hundred and eleven defects were found in simulation. Four were found
on the line. The engineer who built that environment now leads the
hardware-in-the-loop work and would be one of your colleagues.

How we write software
We write design documents for anything that will outlive a sprint, and we
circulate them before the code rather than after it. A document is usually two
to four pages and always has a section on what we decided not to do. Code review
is mandatory, unhurried and not a gate we treat as a formality: the median
review on this team takes a working day and produces comments on more than half
of the changes. We do not measure individual output, and we do not have a story
point. What the team reports upward each period is what shipped and what it
cost, in prose.

Testing and the plant
The hardest thing about our testing is that the real system is a factory. We
cannot deploy to production to see what happens, we cannot roll back a weld, and
the customer's maintenance window is often a single night twice a year. That
constraint shapes everything: our test environments are elaborate because they
have to be, our releases are infrequent and large in a way that would be poor
practice in a web company, and our appetite for a clever abstraction is low. An
engineer joining from a continuously deployed environment usually finds the
first quarter uncomfortable and the second one interesting.

The technology, honestly described
The platform is not new and parts of it are not pretty. The deployment tooling
began as a set of shell scripts in 2016 and became a Go program in 2021; there
are still four scripts. The shared communication library is C++ and carries a
compatibility layer for a protocol version two customers still run and will run
until at least 2029. The observability stack is Prometheus and Grafana with an
in-house exporter for the controllers, which is the piece most people find
interesting and the piece most likely to need rewriting. We mention this because
a candidate who expects a clean slate will be disappointed, and a candidate who
enjoys improving something that is already load-bearing will not.

Career development
The division runs a two-track progression framework: an engineering track that
goes to principal without requiring management, and a management track that
does. Both tracks are published with their expectations, and promotion decisions
are made twice a year by a panel rather than by a single manager. Roughly a
third of the platform team has moved between engineering centres at least once,
usually for between six months and two years, and the group pays relocation
costs for internal moves as well as for hiring.

Working hours and on-call
Standard hours are thirty-eight a week. The platform on-call rotation covers
European business hours only; there is no night rotation for this team, because
the systems it owns are not the ones that run overnight. Project teams with
plant commitments do have night coverage, and platform engineers are sometimes
asked to join a commissioning weekend, which is paid at the collectively agreed
rate and compensated with time in lieu. In the last two years this has averaged
fewer than three weekends per engineer.

Security
All platform code is scanned on every change and reviewed annually by an
external assessor. Engineers hold no standing production credentials; access to
a customer environment is requested, approved and time-boxed, and every session
is recorded. Candidates should expect a background check appropriate to the
sectors we serve, which for this position means employment history and a
criminal records check in the country of employment, and nothing further.

Onboarding
New engineers spend their first week reading rather than writing, which is a
rule we enforce gently and do mean. In the second week the expectation is one
small change shipped end to end, chosen by you from a list the team keeps for
exactly this purpose. There is a named buddy for the first three months and a
written check-in at thirty, sixty and ninety days, the last of which is a
two-way conversation about whether the role is what the advertisement said it
was.
